# Undergraduate Mathematics Curriculum & Self-Study Guide

Below is the structured order of courses, complete with course links, lecture series, and exercise-rich textbooks.

---

## Phase 1: Computational Foundations

### 1. Single-Variable Calculus
* **Prerequisites:** High school algebra, trigonometry, and precalculus.
* **Course & Lectures:** [MIT OCW 18.01SC Single Variable Calculus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/)
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* by James Stewart (or *Calculus* by Gilbert Strang).

### 2. Multivariable Calculus
* **Prerequisites:** Single-Variable Calculus.
* **Course & Lectures:** [MIT OCW 18.02 Multivariable Calculus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02-multivariable-calculus-fall-2007/)
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* by James Stewart.

### 3. Computational Linear Algebra
* **Prerequisites:** Calculus I & basic matrix arithmetic.
* **Course & Lectures:** [MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/) (or [MIT 18.06SC on Open Learning Library](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:OCW+18.06SC+2T2019/course/))
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Introduction to Linear Algebra* by Gilbert Strang.

### 4. Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)
* **Prerequisites:** Multivariable Calculus and Linear Algebra.
* **Course & Lectures:** [MIT OCW 18.03SC Differential Equations](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-03sc-differential-equations-fall-2011/pages/syllabus/syllabus/)
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Differential Equations and Their Applications* by Martin Braun (or *Elementary Differential Equations* by Boyce & DiPrima).

---

## Phase 2: The Transition to Rigor & Proofs

### 5. Mathematical Reasoning & Proofs
* **Prerequisites:** Basic familiarity with calculus and linear systems.
* **Recommended Resource:** *Book of Proof* by Richard Hammack (free open-source textbook with complete odd-problem solutions).
* **Focus:** Propositional logic, direct/contrapositive proofs, contradiction, mathematical induction, and set theory.

### 6. Theoretical Linear Algebra
* **Prerequisites:** Proof fundamentals and computational linear algebra.
* **Lecture Video Overview:** [Course Introduction to MIT 18.06SC Linear Algebra](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hNDFwVVKVk0)
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Linear Algebra Done Right* by Sheldon Axler.

### 7. Probability & Mathematical Statistics
* **Prerequisites:** Multivariable Calculus and Linear Algebra.
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *A First Course in Probability* by Sheldon Ross.

---

## Phase 3: The Core Pure Disciplines

### 8. Real Analysis
* **Prerequisites:** Proof fundamentals, theoretical linear algebra, and multivariable calculus.
* **Curriculum Reference:** [OSSU Mathematics Curriculum Hub](https://github.com/ossu/math)
* **Textbooks & Exercises:**
  * *Understanding Analysis* by Stephen Abbott (intuitive entry point).
  * *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* by Walter Rudin (canonical rigorous standard).

### 9. Abstract Algebra
* **Prerequisites:** Proof fundamentals and theoretical linear algebra.
* **Course & Materials:** [MIT OCW 18.701 Algebra I](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/)
* **Textbooks & Exercises:**
  * *A Book of Abstract Algebra* by Charles C. Pinter (gentle, well-structured problems).
  * *Abstract Algebra* by David S. Dummit and Richard M. Foote (comprehensive standard reference).

---

## Phase 4: Advanced Electives

### 10. Complex Analysis
* **Prerequisites:** Real Analysis and Multivariable Calculus.
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Complex Variables and Applications* by James Ward Brown and Ruel V. Churchill.

### 11. Point-Set Topology
* **Prerequisites:** Real Analysis and Set Theory.
* **Textbook & Exercises:** *Topology* by James Munkres.
